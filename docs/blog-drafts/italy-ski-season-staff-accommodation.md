More often than anywhere else we list. Staff accommodation appears in Italian ski resort adverts far more readily than in Canadian ones, usually bundled with meals, and usually in the same two words.

What those adverts will not tell you is the price. As things stand, not one Italian listing on our board states what the room costs, and not one says whether it comes out of your wages.

## The two words to look for

Nearly every Italian advert that offers housing uses one of these. They are worth recognising on sight, because the English translation we show alongside each listing can flatten them into the same bland phrase.

| Italian | What it means | What to read into it |
|---|---|---|
| **alloggio** | accommodation, lodging | The job comes with somewhere to live. It says nothing about where, what kind, who you share with or what you pay. |
| **vitto e alloggio** | board and lodging | The common pairing, and the stronger offer: meals as well as a room. In hospitality this usually means eating at work, on shift. |
| **vitto** | board, meals | Food only. Worth separating from the room in your head, because the two are often quoted together and withdrawn separately. |
| **alloggio condiviso** · **camera doppia** | shared accommodation · twin room | A room with someone else in it. Rare in the wording we see, which does not make sharing rare — it is the default unless an advert says otherwise. |
| **contratto stagionale** | seasonal contract | The contract type, not a perk. It is the thing that ends in April, and the thing your housing is almost certainly tied to. |

The pairing is the useful signal. An advert offering *vitto e alloggio* is describing a package built for someone arriving from elsewhere, which is the situation you are in.

## What the adverts never say

This is the part worth planning around. Across every open Italian listing we carry:

- **None states a cost.** No weekly rent, no monthly figure, nothing.
- **None says whether it is deducted from your pay.** This is the question that decides what you actually bank, and it is answered nowhere.
- **None states an hourly rate.** A couple quote a monthly or whole-season figure and the rest quote nothing. Italian hospitality pay is set by national collective agreement rather than negotiated advert by advert, so adverts tend to name a contract instead of a number.

That last point is a real difference from Canada, where most listings on our board do quote a rate. It also means the useful question is not "how much an hour?" but "which contract, and which level?" — the published pay tables follow from those two answers, and the [CNEL contract archive](https://www.cnel.it/Archivio-Contratti) is where the official ones live.

## Four questions to send before you accept

Ask all four in one message, in writing. An employer offering a genuine package answers them in a line each.

1. **What is the accommodation, and who else is in it?** A room of your own, a shared room, a bed in staff quarters — and how far from work.
2. **What does it cost, and is it taken out of my pay?** Ask for the figure and the mechanism separately. "Included" and "deducted at source" can both be described as *alloggio incluso*.
3. **Which contract and which level am I on?** That is what fixes your pay, your hours and your notice.
4. **What happens to the room when the contract ends?** Seasonal housing usually ends with the season, sometimes on the same day.

If you want the longer version of that list, [questions to ask before accepting a ski season job](/blog/questions-to-ask-before-accepting-ski-season-job) covers the rest of the offer. And if you are not sure you can legally take an Italian season at all, start with [can you work a ski season in Italy?](/blog/work-a-ski-season-in-italy) — there is a three-month rule in the visa that catches people out.

## Common questions

### Do Italian ski resort jobs include accommodation?

Many of them say they do — a much higher share than the Canadian listings we carry, and usually alongside meals. What almost none of them say is what it costs or whether it is deducted from your pay, so treat "accommodation included" as the start of the conversation rather than the answer.

### What does "vitto e alloggio" mean in a job advert?

Board and lodging: meals as well as a room. It is the standard phrase in Italian hospitality adverts and it signals a package aimed at someone who is moving to the resort for the season. It does not tell you the standard of the room, who you share with, or the price.

### Is staff accommodation in Italy free?

Do not assume so. Not one open Italian listing on our board says its accommodation is free, and not one states a price either — the subject simply is not addressed in writing. Ask for the figure and ask separately whether it is taken out of your wages.

### How much do ski season jobs in Italy pay?

We cannot tell you from our own data, and we would rather say that than guess: effectively none of the open Italian listings state a rate. Pay for hospitality work in Italy is set by national collective agreements with graded levels, so the figure follows from which contract and which level you are hired on. Ask for both, then check the published tables.

### Are the Italian listings on Mountain Connects posted by the employers?

Mostly not. Most are sourced from public adverts, which is also why they are in Italian — we show an English translation and keep the original, because the original is what the employer wrote. You apply using the contact details on the listing.
